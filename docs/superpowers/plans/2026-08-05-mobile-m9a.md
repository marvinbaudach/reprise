# M9a — the surface learns to turn

The first half of the landscape work: the state that must survive a
configuration change, the rule that decides the layout, and the landscape
layouts themselves. The second half — dock mode, the ambient visualiser, the
switch menu and the settings screen — is M9b and depends on this.

Design source: *Reprise Mobile*, frame **17a** (916 × 412 dp), plus the written
brief of 2026-08-05.

## Why now, and not before

Until last night a rotation **stopped the music**: the playback service died with
the activity, so there was no reason to ever turn the phone. M7 fixed that. The
landscape layout stops being decoration the moment turning the device is
something a listener would actually do.

## What is already true, and what is not

- There is **no ViewModel anywhere in the app**. Every piece of state lives in a
  composable and dies with the activity. That is the thing to change.
- There is **no Lyrics screen**. Frame 17a's third view has nothing to lay out;
  it is out of scope, and saying so is part of the work.
- **A favourite is five stars** — the owner settled this, so it does have a
  backend: `rating == 5`. There is still no queue surface and no lyrics screen.
  The brief's landscape transport row lists *favourite, lyrics, queue* after a
  divider; two of those three have nothing behind them and do not appear. Omit
  them deliberately and record why, the way desktop sync and crossfade were.

  The favourite itself needs a decision rather than a control dropped in:

  - **In this layout it may be redundant.** The right-hand column already carries
    the rating row — five tappable stars, right there. A separate button that
    writes five next to a widget that already writes five is two ways to say one
    thing. Decide whether it earns its place *here*, and say why. (Dock mode is
    the case where it clearly does: M9b removes the rating row entirely, and then
    one tap for "keep this" is the whole point.)
  - **Un-favouriting is a data question, not a toggle.** If favourite means five
    stars, what does turning it off mean? Zero throws away a rating the listener
    may have set deliberately; four invents one they never gave. Whatever you
    choose, it must not quietly destroy a rating — this codebase has a standing
    rule about that, and it has been broken twice.

## The work

### 1. State that survives the turn

Introduce a `ViewModel` and move into it everything a configuration change must
not lose:

- the library list's **scroll position**
- which tab is selected, and the search text if one is open
- an **in-flight scrub gesture** — this is the hard one. A drag that is
  interrupted by a rotation must continue against the new layout rather than
  snapping back or committing a seek nobody asked for. Decide what "continue"
  means when the bar changes width mid-gesture, and say why.

The rule to write down: **what survives a configuration change lives in the
ViewModel; what is genuinely per-composition stays in the composition.** A
ViewModel that accumulates everything is its own kind of mess.

Do **not** put the playing track there. M7 established where that lives: the
playback session owns it, the surface asks. Nothing changes about that.

### 2. `WindowSizeClass`, not `Configuration.orientation`

The layout decision is made from the window size class. Orientation is the wrong
question: a folded and an unfolded foldable are both "landscape" at wildly
different sizes, and a tablet in portrait has more room than a phone in
landscape. 916 × 412 dp is **expanded width at compact height** — wide and short,
with roughly 260 dp of usable vertical space, and that is what the rules below
are derived from.

### 3. The three rules the brief gives, applied

- The `NavigationBar` becomes a `NavigationRail` at the left edge, **80 dp**
  wide, entries vertically centred, the active one carrying a **56 × 32 dp**
  pill.
- Anything stacked is laid side by side.
- Lists get **two columns** rather than longer rows.

**Library.** Row height 72 → **64 dp**, two columns with a 16 dp gap — eight
titles visible instead of three. Top app bar to **52 dp**. The mini player spans
the **content** width and not the rail: the rail belongs to navigation alone.

**Now Playing.** Cover **326 dp** on the left, vertically centred. On the right a
column with title, rating, seek and transport. The transport sits at the bottom
edge and takes the mode actions with it — previous, next, play, shuffle, repeat —
because in landscape the width is there and stacking them would waste it. Play
button **64 dp** rather than 76, because the vertical room is not.

### 4. Nothing may regress in portrait

Portrait is the shipped layout and it has been measured on a device: 72 dp rows,
the 80 dp navigation bar with its system inset, the 28 dp rounded play button at
240 px, the mini player at 72 dp. The landscape work must not move any of it.
Say how you know it did not.

## What this package does not touch

Dock mode, the ambient visualiser, the visualiser switch menu, the settings
screen. They are M9b. The seek bar's redesign and two of the three visualisers
need spectrogram data that does not exist yet; they are not in either.

## Decisions made in the implementation

The lifetime rule is now explicit: **state that must survive an activity
recreation lives in `MobileSurfaceViewModel`; state that is genuinely owned by
one composition stays in that composition.** The ViewModel owns the selected
tab, an open search and its text, one scroll anchor per library list, the open
Now Playing/settings overlays, and the current scrub interaction. Loaded
catalog windows, transient errors and messages, playback-settings snapshots,
menus, predictive-back animation progress, cover requests, and drawing state
remain local. The playing track is not in the ViewModel; the service still
publishes its stable identity and the replacement activity reloads that row.

An interrupted scrub keeps the intended **time**, not the old bar's pixel
coordinate. While it is active, playback ticks cannot move it. The replacement
bar therefore draws the same timestamp at the corresponding fraction of its new
width. Activity destruction does not release the gesture and cannot seek; after
the turn, the listener must touch the new bar and release deliberately before a
seek is committed. This is the narrowest meaning of “continue” that does not
invent a pointer event Android cancelled with the old window.

The responsive branch is `WindowWidthSizeClass.Expanded` together with
`WindowHeightSizeClass.Compact`, calculated by Material 3's
`calculateWindowSizeClass`. It does not read `Configuration.orientation`.

The landscape Now Playing row deliberately has no separate favourite button.
The five-star row already writes five in one tap, so a second adjacent control
would duplicate the same action. More importantly, a favourite toggle has no
honest inverse: zero destroys a deliberate rating and four invents one. Dock
mode may add the one-way “set five” action in M9b when the rating row is absent,
but M9a does not create a destructive toggle. Lyrics and queue actions are also
absent because neither surface exists; there is no placeholder control and no
claim that either action works.

The activity-recreation tests run `MainActivity.onCreate`, `onStart`, the real
service bind and `onServiceConnected`, the production Compose surface, and
`ActivityScenario.recreate`; only the JNI library edge is replaced. The pure
size/state tests run the Material 3 classifier and ViewModel methods directly.
The geometry test measures both arrangements through that activity path: the
portrait 64/72/72/80 dp frame and 364/80 dp Now Playing geometry first, then the
52/64 dp, two-column, 80 dp rail, content-only mini player and 326/64 dp
wide-short geometry after recreation. This is how portrait non-regression is
known without claiming a device rendering pass.

## Decisions made after review

The anchor survived the turn and the rows did not. `onCreate` restores one
window of 200; everything the listener paged in lived in the composition and
died with it, so an index of 211 landed a replacement activity on row 200 — the
last item of the reloaded window — without a word. **The paged-in windows now
live in the ViewModel too.** The argument for keeping rows out of durable state
is `TransactionTooLargeException`, which is a Binder limit on a parcel crossing
a process boundary; a ViewModel crosses none, the rows are already in this
process, and the alternative — anchoring by identity and paging forward until
the row appears — buys freshness with a run of synchronous library reads on the
main thread, under the lock a scan holds, every time the phone is turned.

The staleness that buys is bounded on purpose. The windows are kept against the
**catalog's shape** — how many titles, albums and artists the first window
reports — so a scan that finished while the screen was being replaced drops
them. Counts and not the rows themselves, because a play just finished changes a
row's play count without changing what is in the library.

When the rows cannot be taken up, the anchor **falls back to the top** rather
than to the last row that happens to exist. Row 200 claims a place was restored;
the top reads as the reset it is.

The anchor's sub-row offset is kept as a **fraction of its row**, not in pixels:
a title row is 72 dp stacked and 64 dp wide-short, so the same pixel count is a
different place in each. It is applied on the list's first layout, which is when
a fraction becomes a pixel count.

The rail now adds the **start** system-bar inset to its 80 dp, the way the
bottom bar already adds the bottom one — turned sideways that inset is where a
three-button navigation bar goes. Untested and unmeasured: Robolectric reports
no insets, so this needs the device.

The `navigationRailIndicator*` constants are **gone**. They were declared and
asserted but never handed to a composable; the pill is right only because
Material 3's default is the 56 × 32 dp that 17a asks for, and a test of an
unread constant proves that a constant equals itself.

## Proof

Robolectric drives configuration changes, so a rotation is testable in the
ordinary JVM gate — and M7 left a warning about how that goes wrong. Its rotation
test stayed **green while the device failed**, because it wired `BrowseScreen`
directly and never entered `MainActivity.onCreate`/`onServiceConnected`. A test
whose claim is about surviving a configuration change must go through the path a
configuration change actually takes. Say which path each of your tests runs.

Every test mutation-proven: break the mechanism, show the real red output,
restore it, show green. A test that also passes without the fix does not count.

## Nothing on the owner's screen — absolute

No window, no dialog, no browser, no app in the foreground; nothing takes focus.
**No emulator, no `adb`, no launching the app** — the device pass is mine, and
for this package it is the one that matters, because Robolectric recreates an
activity without ever turning a phone. Headless only: compilation, unit tests,
gates.
